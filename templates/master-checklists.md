# Master Checklists (printable)

Every end-of-chapter checklist from *Computing Projects from Concept to
Defense*, compiled into one per-stage runbook. Print it, pin it above your
desk, tick as you go.

These are prompts for work the book explains. When a line is not
self-explanatory, the chapter it came from carries the reasoning.

## Frame

- [ ] I have three candidate topics, scored for feasibility.
- [ ] My surviving topic has a one-sentence problem statement—I can complete “my project shows that…”.
- [ ] I have two to four sub-questions or measurable objectives.
- [ ] I know whether my degree's bar is novelty of application or of knowledge.
- [ ] I have identified every column of the domain-norms table my project occupies.
- [ ] My proposal names an evaluation method, not just a build.
- [ ] My proposal sketches a timeline and names its top two or three risks.
- [ ] I know which approvals, if any, gate my start—and their lead times.

## Review

- [ ] My literature matrix covers the nearest competing approaches, the principal methodological alternatives, and the sources that justify my baseline (sufficiency, not a count, is the test).
- [ ] Every entry records its evaluation method, not just its idea.
- [ ] I searched beyond papers: repositories, standards, leaderboards.
- [ ] My synthesis is organized by theme, not paper-by-paper.
- [ ] My synthesis ends in one explicit gap sentence.
- [ ] I have named the baseline my evaluation must beat.

## Plan

- [ ] My “must” features are done-able by the midpoint.
- [ ] My “won't” list is written down, not implied.
- [ ] I have 4–6 milestones tied to deliverables, not activities.
- [ ] My risk register has triggers and fallbacks, not just risks.
- [ ] I review the register weekly and act on fired triggers.
- [ ] I know which approvals gate my timeline and applied in week two.

## Build

- [ ] My context and container diagrams pass the one-minute rule.
- [ ] I have kept a decision log since the first build week.
- [ ] My repository has a README that gets a stranger running.
- [ ] My tests protect the claims, and I can say why.
- [ ] I have a demonstrable vertical slice by the midpoint.
- [ ] My use of AI coding tools is disclosed, and I can walk through every function.

## Evaluate

- [ ] Every claim maps to a method in my claims–evidence table.
- [ ] I chose two or three methods deliberately, not by habit.
- [ ] I repeated runs and reported variance, not single numbers.
- [ ] (AI) I have audited for train/test leakage and included a trivial baseline.
- [ ] (Security) I report precision at a realistic base rate, not accuracy alone.
- [ ] My limitations section pre-empts the obvious objections.
- [ ] I know what I will write if the headline result is negative.

## Write

- [ ] My chapter outline maps every chapter to existing raw material.
- [ ] I can state my thesis argument in one paragraph, and each chapter advances it.
- [ ] My introduction's promises and conclusion's answers mirror exactly.
- [ ] Every figure and table is captioned, described, interpreted, and connected to a claim.
- [ ] My citations all resolve to real sources I actually used.
- [ ] My thesis source is version-controlled, backed up, and compiles daily.
- [ ] My schedule works backwards from the deadline with named buffers.

## Defend

- [ ] I know my format, my time slot, and who will be in the room.
- [ ] My talk spends more time on evidence than on implementation.
- [ ] My demo has a script, staged data, and a tested recording as backup.
- [ ] My answer bank covers the eight standard questions plus my known weak points.
- [ ] I have rehearsed aloud three times, including one mock defense.
- [ ] I know how I will say “I don't know” when it is true.

## After the Defense

- [ ] I have made an honest go/no-go publication decision with my supervisor.
- [ ] If go: venue chosen, authorship agreed, compression scheduled.
- [ ] My repository is history-scrubbed, licensed, and README-fronted.
- [ ] I can tell the project as a two-minute interview arc.
- [ ] (Security) my publication plan respects the disclosure timeline.
- [ ] My successor could start from my handover notes, not from zero.
